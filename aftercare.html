Preparation & Aftercare

General guidance for before and after your session. Every tattoo heals a little differently — always follow the specific instructions Megan gives you at your appointment.

A note before anything else: this page is general guidance. Megan's personal, written aftercare instructions given at your appointment always take priority over anything here — every skin type, placement, and design heals a little differently.

01

Preparing for Your Appointment

Get a good night's sleep beforehand and try to come in well-rested — tattoos are easier to sit for (and heal better) when your body isn't already run down. Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours before your session, as it thins the blood and can affect how your skin takes ink.

If you take any regular medication, keep taking it as prescribed unless your doctor says otherwise. Avoid blood thinners like aspirin or ibuprofen in the 24–48 hours before your appointment if you're able to (check with your doctor first).

02

Eating & Hydration

Eat a full meal within a couple of hours of your appointment — an empty stomach and a tattoo needle are not friends. Drink plenty of water in the days leading up to your session; well-hydrated skin tattoos more easily and tends to heal faster.

Bring a snack and water bottle for longer sessions. It's completely normal to take breaks during a longer piece.

03

What to Wear

Wear something loose and comfortable that gives easy access to the area being tattooed, ideally in a color you won't mind getting a little ink on. For larger pieces on the ribs, back, or thigh, dressing in layers you can adjust makes for a more comfortable session.

04

Cleaning Your New Tattoo

Leave the initial bandage on for the amount of time Megan recommends (typically a few hours). When you remove it, gently wash the tattoo with clean hands using a fragrance-free, mild soap and lukewarm water — no washcloths or loofahs. Pat dry with a clean paper towel; avoid rubbing with fabric towels.

Wash your tattoo two to three times a day during the first week, or any time it feels tacky or has plasma/ink residue on the surface.

05

Moisturizing

Once clean and fully dry, apply a thin layer of the fragrance-free healing balm or lotion Megan recommends. A little goes a long way — over-moisturizing can trap moisture and slow healing just as much as under-moisturizing. The tattoo should look matte, not shiny or greasy.

06

Things to Avoid

While healing, avoid: soaking the tattoo (baths, pools, hot tubs, the ocean), direct sun exposure, tight clothing that rubs the area, picking or scratching at scabs or peeling skin, and any strenuous exercise that causes excess sweating over the piece for the first week or two.

Skip re-applying sunscreen directly to a fresh tattoo until it's fully healed — once healed, sunscreen is essential for keeping the tattoo looking sharp for years to come.

07

The Healing Timeline

Every tattoo and every body is a little different, but generally: the surface heals in about 2–3 weeks, with peeling and mild flaking around days 5–10. Deeper healing continues beneath the surface for up to a couple of months, during which the tattoo may look slightly hazy before settling into its final, crisp appearance.

If you notice unusual redness, warmth, swelling, or discharge beyond the first few days, reach out to Megan — and a medical professional if anything seems concerning.
